**The second great voice of Taoism**

If the Tao Te Ching is a short book of poems, the Zhuangzi is a long book of stories. It is named after Zhuang Zhou, a thinker who lived in the 4th century BCE. Scholars think only part of the book, the first seven "inner chapters," comes from him; the rest was added by later writers.

**Famous stories**

The Zhuangzi teaches through images you remember. A cook carves an ox so easily that his knife never dulls, because he follows the natural spaces in the joints. A man dreams he is a butterfly and wakes up unsure which one he really is. A huge, twisted tree survives because it is too "useless" for anyone to cut down.

**How it relates to the Tao Te Ching**

Both books speak of the Tao, of acting without forcing (wu wei), and of the value of what looks weak or useless. The Tao Te Ching often speaks to rulers. The Zhuangzi speaks more to the single person who wants to be free inside.

This site focuses on the Tao Te Ching, but you will sometimes see an image from the Zhuangzi in the journal, always named as such.
