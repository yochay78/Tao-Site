**The book's favorite teacher**

If the Tao Te Ching had to pick one image to explain itself, it would be water. Water shows up again and again, and each time it teaches something the book cannot say directly about the Tao.

**The highest good**

Chapter 8 opens:

*The highest good is like water.*
*Water serves myriad things*
*and never competes.*
*It settles where others refuse to go,*
*and so it is near the Tao.*

Water does not choose the high places. It goes down, into the cracks and the valleys that nobody wants. And that is exactly why everything grows near it.

**Soft, yet unbeatable**

Chapter 78 makes the strongest claim: "Nothing in the world is softer or weaker than water, yet nothing beats it at attacking the hard and strong." Chapter 43 says the same in other words: "The softest thing in the world rides over the hardest."

This is not magic. It is patience. Water does not win by force. It wins by never stopping and never breaking.

**Staying low**

Chapter 66 turns water into a lesson about leading: "Why are rivers and seas kings of the hundred valleys? Because they are good at staying low." Everything flows toward what is low.

**Knowing it, living it**

The hardest line about water is in chapter 78: "Everyone in the world knows it, yet no one can practice it." We all know that pushing harder often makes things worse. Being like water is the practice of actually stopping. It is wu wei in its most natural form.
