**A small word with a big life**

Qi (氣) first meant breath, air or vapor. Over time Chinese thinkers used it for the energy that moves through the body and the whole world. In the Tao Te Ching the word is rare. It appears in only three chapters, and each time the point is the same: softness, not force.

**Breathe soft as a newborn**

Chapter 10 asks a series of quiet questions, among them: "Can you breathe soft as a newborn child?" A baby's breath is not trained or controlled. It is relaxed and whole. The book keeps returning to the newborn as a picture of life at its fullest.

**Breath and harmony**

Chapter 42 describes how the Tao gives birth to everything, and then: "The myriad things carry yin and embrace yang, and blend their breath into harmony." Qi is what lets the two sides of [yin and yang](/glossary/yin-and-yang/) live together instead of fighting.

**Don't force the breath**

Chapter 55 praises the newborn again: "It cries all day and does not grow hoarse. Its harmony is at its fullest." Then it warns: "To force life is ill-omened. To drive the breath by will is to be violent." Pushing your energy, your body or your life past its natural pace is the opposite of [wu wei](/glossary/wu-wei/).

**Qi in daily life**

You don't need a special technique to use this. When you notice yourself straining, let the breath soften for a moment, like a baby's. Let the body set the pace. See [How to Stop Overthinking](/journal/how-to-stop-overthinking/) for a simple way to come back to the breath.
