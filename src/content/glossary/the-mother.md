**Mother of myriad things**

Chapter 1 opens by saying the Tao that can be spoken is not the lasting Tao. Then: "The Nameless, root of heaven and earth. The named, mother of myriad things." Once we start naming, we meet the Tao as a mother: the one everything is born from.

**The mother of the world**

Chapter 25 describes "something formless and complete" that was "born before heaven and earth," moving everywhere without tiring, and says: "It may be the mother of all under heaven." Chapter 52 says it plainly: "The world had a beginning. That beginning is the mother of the world."

**The dark mother**

Chapter 6 links the mother with [the valley](/glossary/the-valley/): "The valley spirit never dies. Call it the dark mother." It is "faint, unbroken," and "use it, and it never tires." This is the quiet, hidden, endlessly giving side of the Tao, close to [emptiness](/glossary/emptiness/).

**Know the children, return to the mother**

Chapter 52 gives the practice: "Know the mother, and you know the children. Know the children, and return to the mother." Life is full of children: tasks, plans, people, worries. Enjoy them, but keep [returning](/glossary/returning/) to the source. "Then to the end of your life you are not in danger."

**Nursing at the Mother**

In chapter 20 the speaker feels different from everyone around him, and ends: "I prize nursing at the Mother." While others chase, he stays close to what feeds him. Chapter 59 uses the same image for steady leadership: "Hold the mother of a country, and you can last long."

**Why a mother?**

A mother gives life and keeps giving without owning. Chapter 10 says it of the sage: "to bear and to nourish, to make and not to own." Many readers see this as the book's quiet answer to power that controls. See [How to Start Over](/journal/starting-over-tao/).
