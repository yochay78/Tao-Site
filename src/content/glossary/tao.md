**A word for what has no name**

The Tao Te Ching begins by admitting the problem:

*The Tao that can be spoken*
*is not the lasting Tao.*
*A name that can be named*
*is not the lasting name.*

So the Tao is not a concept to master. It is a pointer toward the way life moves before we add our ideas to it: the way water finds the low place, the way seasons turn, the way a wound heals without being told how.

**How the book describes it**

Chapter 25 calls it "something formless and complete" that "was born before heaven and earth." Lao Tzu adds: "I do not know its name. I call it the Tao." Chapter 32 says the Tao "is always nameless," and compares it to valley streams flowing toward the sea. Chapter 42 describes it as the beginning of everything: "The Tao gives birth to one. One gives birth to two. Two gives birth to three. Three gives birth to the myriad things."

**Why it matters in practice**

The Tao is not only a cosmic idea. It is also a question you can ask in an ordinary moment: am I moving with this situation, or forcing it? Chapter 51 describes the Tao as something that "gives birth and does not possess. Acts and does not depend. Leads and does not rule." That is also a picture of how a person can live.

On this site we always write "Tao" rather than translating it, because any English word makes it smaller than it is.
