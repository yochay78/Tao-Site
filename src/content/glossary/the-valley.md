**Low, empty, alive**

A valley is the lowest place in the land. Rain runs into it, rivers gather in it, and life grows there. The Tao Te Ching keeps coming back to this picture because it shows a strength that does not push.

**The valley spirit**

Chapter 6 is only a few lines long:

*The valley spirit never dies.*
*Call it the dark mother.*
*The gate of the dark mother*
*is the root of heaven and earth.*

The valley is alive because it is empty. A full valley would be a mountain. Its openness is its power.

**Be the valley of the world**

Chapter 28 turns the image into a practice: "Know the male, keep to the female, and be the valley of the world." You do not have to give up strength. You know it. But you rest in openness. Then, the chapter says, "you return to the infant," and later, to the uncarved block.

**The feminine and staying low**

Chapter 61 links the valley to the feminine: "The female overcomes the male by stillness, by lying low." Chapter 66 says rivers and seas are kings of the hundred valleys "because they are good at staying low." Like water, the valley wins by being low, not by climbing.

**A quieter kind of greatness**

Chapter 41 says "High virtue seems like a valley." Real goodness often looks like nothing much: open, quiet, receiving. Chapter 15 describes the people of old who walked the Tao the same way: "Open, like a valley."
