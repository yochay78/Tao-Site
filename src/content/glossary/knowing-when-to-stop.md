**A teaching about limits**

The Tao Te Ching returns again and again to one simple idea: every action has a natural limit. Go past it, and the thing you wanted turns against you. The Chinese phrase is 知止 (zhi zhi), "knowing stopping," knowing when to stop.

**The cup and the blade**

Chapter 9 opens with two images:

*Fill a cup to the brim*
*and it is better to stop.*
*Sharpen a blade to its edge*
*and it will not last.*

A full cup spills at the first step. A blade sharpened too thin chips. Work, ambition, even kindness can be like this. The last bit of "more" is often what breaks it.

**When the work is done**

The same chapter ends: "When the work is done, step back. Such is the way of [heaven](/glossary/heaven/)." Spring does not stay into summer to make sure everyone noticed it. Stepping back is not giving up. It is completing.

**Stopping keeps you safe**

Chapter 32 says that once things are named and divided, you must "know when to stop. Knowing when to stop, you avoid danger." Chapter 44 asks: "Gain or loss, which is the harm?" and answers: "Know what is enough and you are not shamed. Know when to stop and you are not in danger. So you can last long."

**In daily life**

Ending the workday while you still have something left. Leaving the argument after you have said it once. Not adding a fourth point when two were enough. This is a practical side of [wu wei](/glossary/wu-wei/) and of thrift, one of [the three treasures](/glossary/three-treasures/). It also links to contentment, which the journal explores in [knowing when enough is enough](/journal/knowing-when-enough-is-enough/).
