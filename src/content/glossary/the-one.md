**The first step out of the Tao**

The Chinese word is 一 (yi), simply "one." In the Tao Te Ching it is more than a number. It is the first wholeness that comes from the [Tao](/glossary/tao/), before the world divides into opposites.

**One, two, three, the myriad things**

Chapter 42 gives the famous sequence:

*The Tao gives birth to one.*
*One gives birth to two.*
*Two gives birth to three.*
*Three gives birth to the myriad things.*

The book does not explain the numbers, and scholars debate them. A common reading is that two is [yin and yang](/glossary/yin-and-yang/), and three is the harmony born when they meet. The next line supports this: things "carry yin and embrace yang, and blend their breath into harmony."

**Attaining the One**

Chapter 39 shows what the One does:

*Heaven attained the One and became clear.*
*Earth attained the One and became calm.*

Each thing stays itself by being whole. "All this came from the One." Without it, the chapter says, [heaven](/glossary/heaven/) would crack and earth would break.

**Embracing the One**

For a person, the One is about inner wholeness. Chapter 22: "So the Nameless embraces the One and becomes the pattern for the world." Chapter 10 asks: "Can you hold body and soul as one and not be torn apart?" And chapter 14 describes the Tao as unseen, unheard and untouched: "They blend and become one."

**In daily life**

Much of our stress comes from being split: wanting one thing, doing another, fearing a third. Embracing the One means coming back to one place, one breath, one next step. See [How to Start Over](/journal/starting-over-tao/) for chapter 22 and the movement of returning.
