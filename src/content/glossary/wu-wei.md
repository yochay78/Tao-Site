**Not doing, or not forcing?**

Wu wei is probably the most famous idea in the Tao Te Ching, and the most misunderstood. "Non-doing" sounds like laziness. But the book describes a sage who acts, works, leads and builds. What he does not do is strain against the grain of things.

Chapter 37 puts it as a paradox:

*The Tao does nothing,*
*and yet nothing is left undone.*

Nature does not hurry or push, and yet rivers carve valleys and trees grow tall.

**Less and less**

Chapter 48 shows wu wei as a process of dropping, not adding: "In pursuit of learning, every day something is added. In pursuit of the Tao, every day something is dropped. Less and less until you reach doing nothing. When nothing is done, nothing is left undone."

Chapter 63 makes it practical: "Act without acting. Work without working." And: "Plan the difficult while it is easy. Do the great while it is small."

**What it feels like**

Wu wei is the difference between swimming against a current and swimming with it. You still move your arms. You just stop wasting your strength on the fight. Chapter 2 describes the sage this way: he "acts without striving and teaches without words... He works and does not cling."
