**Two sides that need each other**

Yin and yang are a very old pair in Chinese thought. Yin is the shaded side: dark, soft, low, still, receiving. Yang is the sunny side: bright, firm, high, active, giving. Neither is better. Each makes the other possible.

The Tao Te Ching names them only once, in chapter 42:

*The myriad things carry yin and embrace yang,*
*and blend their breath into harmony.*

**The idea runs through the whole book**

Even where the words do not appear, the pattern is everywhere. Chapter 2: "Hard and easy complete each other. Long and short measure each other. High and low lean on each other." Chapter 40 says, "Returning is the movement of the Tao."

**Why the book leans toward yin**

Most of us are trained to value the yang side: pushing, winning, shining. The Tao Te Ching keeps restoring the balance. Chapter 28 says: "Know the male, keep to the female, and be the valley of the world." It does not say give up strength. It says know it, and rest in the softer place.

The black-and-white circle many people know as the yin-yang symbol comes from later Chinese tradition. It does not appear in the Tao Te Ching itself.
