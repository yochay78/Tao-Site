**Nothing gives the use**

Chapter 11 is one of the clearest pictures in the whole book:

*Thirty spokes meet at the hub,*
*yet the emptiness at the center*
*turns the wheel.*

The same goes for a clay pot and a room. We look at the solid parts, but the empty part is what we use. The chapter ends: "So Something gives the form and Nothing gives the use."

**Two Chinese words**

Two words carry this idea. *Wu* (無) means "not having" or non-being; it is the "Nothing" of chapter 11 and the "non-being" of chapter 40: "Being is born from non-being." *Xu* (虛) means empty or open, as in chapter 16: "Reach the utmost emptiness."

**The Tao is an empty bowl**

Chapter 4 says: "The Tao is an empty bowl. Use it, and it is never full." Chapter 5 compares heaven and earth to a bellows: "Empty, yet it never runs dry." Emptiness here is not weakness. It is endless capacity, like [the valley](/glossary/the-valley/).

**Empty so you can be filled**

Chapter 22 turns this into a law of life: "Be hollow, and you will be filled." Chapter 45 adds: "Great fullness seems empty." A full cup cannot receive anything. A crowded mind cannot see clearly. Emptiness makes room for what comes next, which is why it is so close to [returning](/glossary/returning/).

**In daily life**

Leave some space in your day, a pause in a conversation, a minute with nothing to do. That space is not wasted. Like the hub of the wheel, it is what keeps everything else turning.
