**Wood before it becomes a thing**

Picture a block of wood before anyone has carved it. It is not yet a bowl, a chair or a statue. It could still become anything. That is the image the Tao Te Ching uses for simplicity and original nature.

Chapter 15 describes the old masters as "plain, like uncarved wood." Chapter 19 gives it as a practice: "Show the plain. Embrace the uncarved. Want little. Own little."

**Returning, not adding**

Chapter 28 is the fullest picture. After knowing honor but keeping to the humble place, "you return to the uncarved block." Then it adds:

*The uncarved block is cut into tools.*
*The Nameless uses them to rule.*
*The great carving does not cut.*

We all get shaped: by jobs, roles, opinions and habits. The Tao Te Ching does not say this is wrong. It says remember the wood under the shape.

**Simplicity that calms**

Chapter 32 links the uncarved to the Tao itself: "The Tao is always nameless. Uncarved, simple, small, yet nothing in the world can rule it." And chapter 37 says the "nameless uncarved block is free of desire. Free of desire, there is stillness, and the world settles by itself."
