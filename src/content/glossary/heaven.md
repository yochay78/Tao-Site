**The sky and the order above us**

Heaven, 天 (tian) in Chinese, appears again and again in the Tao Te Ching, often as "heaven and earth." It means the sky, and the natural order that the sky stands for: seasons, day and night, the steady movement of things. In older Chinese thought, heaven was often seen as a ruling power above the world. The Tao Te Ching keeps the word, but quietly moves it down one step.

**Heaven follows the Tao**

Chapter 25 says the Tao "was born before heaven and earth," and then gives a short ladder:

*People follow earth.*
*Earth follows heaven.*
*Heaven follows the Tao.*
*The Tao follows what is so of itself.*

So heaven is great, but not the greatest. Above it is the [Tao](/glossary/tao/), and the Tao follows only [ziran](/glossary/ziran/), being so of itself.

**The way of heaven**

When the book says "the way of heaven," it usually means how nature acts without forcing. Chapter 9: "When the work is done, step back. Such is the way of heaven." Chapter 73: "The way of heaven does not strive, yet wins." Chapter 77 compares it to drawing a bow: the high is pressed down, the low is raised. And the last chapter, 81: "The way of heaven benefits and does not harm."

**Not sentimental, yet not harsh**

Chapter 5 says "Heaven and earth are not sentimental." Rain falls on every field. Heaven does not play favorites, and the [sage](/glossary/the-nameless/) learns that same even-handedness. Chapter 7 adds why heaven lasts: "They last because they do not live for themselves."

**In daily life**

The way of heaven is a model for a calm life: do the work, step back, don't strive to win. See [The Tao Te Ching on Fear and Courage](/journal/tao-te-ching-on-fear/) for how chapter 73 speaks to courage.
