Chapter 78 of the Tao Te Ching says that nothing in the world is softer or weaker than water, and nothing is better at wearing down the hard and strong. Everyone knows this, the text adds, and no one puts it into practice.

That last line is the real point. We all know the idea. We rarely live by it.

**Rigid things break**

A dry branch snaps in the wind. A green one bends and returns. Chapter 76 makes the same point about people: the stiff and unbending are close to death; the soft and yielding are close to life.

**What yielding actually is**

Yielding is not weakness. It is choosing not to spend strength on resistance that accomplishes nothing. The person who can let an insult pass, or let a plan change, keeps their strength for what matters.

**Becoming unshakable**

Unshakable does not mean hard. A rock gets chipped and cracked. Water cannot be damaged by a blow; it simply moves around it, and keeps going.

That is the thread of *The Taoist Secret to Becoming Unshakable*: how to practice this in a real, loud, crowded life.
