The Tao Te Ching does not tell you to stop grieving. It tells you that everything moves and changes, including grief itself. Chapter 23 says: "A whirlwind does not last the morning. A downpour does not last the day." The storm is real. It is also passing.

When we lose someone, or a marriage, a home, a version of our life, people often rush to comfort us with answers. The Tao Te Ching is quieter than that. It does not explain loss away. It shows how all things move, and lets you find your place in that movement.

**A whirlwind does not last the morning**

Chapter 23 begins with weather:

*A whirlwind does not last the morning.*
*A downpour does not last the day.*
*Who makes these? Heaven and earth.*
*If even heaven and earth cannot make things last,*
*how much less can people?*

Read this two ways. First: nothing we love can be made to last forever. Not even [heaven](/glossary/heaven/) and earth can do that. Second: the wave of pain that hits you at three in the morning is also weather. It comes with full force, and it does not stay at full force.

The same chapter has a strange line: "Become one with loss, and loss welcomes you." One gentle reading is this: when you stop fighting the fact of the loss, it stops fighting you. You do not have to like it. You only have to stop pretending it did not happen.

**Each goes back to its root**

Chapter 16 is the Tao Te Ching's clearest picture of life and death:

*The myriad things rise and fall*
*and I watch them return.*
*They flourish,*
*and each goes back to its root.*

Leaves grow, turn color and fall, and they feed the soil that grows the next leaves. In the Tao, this is called [returning](/glossary/returning/). It is not a punishment and not a mistake. It is the basic movement of [the myriad things](/glossary/myriad-things/).

The chapter ends with a line worth reading slowly: "The Tao is lasting. The body dies and there is no danger." What we lost was a form. What the form came from does not go anywhere.

**To die and not be lost**

Chapter 33 closes with two lines that many people in grief find steadying:

*To die and not be lost*
*is to live long.*

The people we love leave traces in us: a way of laughing, a sentence we still hear, a kindness we now pass on. In that sense they are not lost. A life is long not only by its years, but by how much of it keeps moving through others.

**Not sentimental, but not cold**

Chapter 5 says "heaven and earth are not sentimental. They treat the myriad things like [straw dogs](/glossary/straw-dogs/)." In grief this line can sound harsh. But it is honest. Nature plays no favorites. Loss is not a sign that you were singled out or that you did something wrong. It comes to every life, and you are not alone in it.

And the Tao is never asking you to harden. Chapter 76 says:

*So the hard and stiff are followers of death.*
*The soft and weak are followers of life.*

Tears are soft. Talking about the person you miss is soft. Letting a hard day be a hard day is soft. Grief that is allowed to flow tends to move. Grief that is locked away tends to turn into stiffness: in the body, in the face, in the heart.

The [Zhuangzi](/glossary/zhuangzi/), the other great Taoist classic, tells a famous story: when Zhuangzi's wife died, a friend found him drumming and singing. Zhuangzi said he had mourned at first, and then saw her death as part of the change of seasons. Many people miss the first part. He grieved first.

**A small practice for heavy days**

1. When a wave comes, name it as weather: "This is a downpour. It does not last the day."
2. Let it be soft. Cry if you need to. Don't make yourself hard to get through it.
3. Notice one trace the person or the old life left in you, and let it live today.
4. Go outside and watch something return: falling leaves, evening light, the tide.
5. Keep only the next small task. Grief has its own timing, and the Tao does not hurry it.

Grief is the shape love takes after a loss. The Tao does not take it away. It gives it room, and trusts it to move, the way water finds its way back to the sea.

If you need a place inside you that the storm doesn't reach, the book below is a quiet practice for coming back to the root.
